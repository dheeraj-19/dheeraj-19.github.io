Project brief:

Ask:

Focusing on a core user group, develop accessibility features for a product in Microsoft’s product portfolio. Explore different areas and learn about a group of users that often get left behind.
Your chosen user group must be currently underserved by the offerings in your chosen area. The features that you build should directly address the real problems currently experienced by your users. The solutions must fit within the Microsoft ecosystem, the business and product priorities, and the industry space.
Put simply, help make Microsoft products more inclusive

Full Project Brief


Product and Usergroup:

We started the project by finalizing the Microsoft Product and the user group that we wanted to focus on.
So, we decided to choose Microsoft Teams, and the underserved community of color-blind users

Why Teams and Why Color Blindness?
Microsoft Teams is a popular collaboration platform used by millions worldwide. However, there are still opportunities to enhance accessibility, particularly for those with vision impairments like color blindness.

Colorblind individuals have difficulty distinguishing between certain colors, and this can affect their experience with software and applications that rely heavily on color-coded information or interfaces.

Around 10% of Teams' 280 million users, or 28 million people, are colorblind. The problems they face include lack of customization, low contrast, inaccessible data visualizations, and reliance on color cues.
Now how did we come up with this number, since we didn't have direct access to Microsoft Teams data, this was acheived via the Discovery phase


Discovery:

In this phase, we studied the product i.e. Teams, and our user group i.e. color-blind users in much detail.
Few statistics that we found were:
- The estimated number of people with color blindness is around 300 million worldwide.


- Worldwide Users of Microsoft Teams: 280 Million
- 1 in 8 men and 1 in 200 women are colorblind
- We also found that 72% of Teams users are men and the rest 28% are women.
- So by doing some calculations, we found out:
Estimated Color-Blind Users in Microsoft Teams: 28 million (10%)



Identifying problems faced by color-blind users while using Microsoft Teams:

The next step was to find out the problems that color-blind users face, now this was a challenge since we didn't have access to Microsoft's historical data and it was even difficult to find color-blind people who use Teams.

Featured - Full-Stack | Hackathon | Clustering Combating Food Insecurity

VIEW DEMO Git Presentation


Food insecurity is a pressing issue that affects communities across the globe, and the Atlanta Community Food Bank is no exception. The Food Bank faced several challenges in ensuring frequent, consistent, and convenient delivery of equitable food to partners. Partners had to order a minimum of 6,000 pounds of food for delivery, which sometimes led to missed deliveries or additional costs for renting vehicles. Additionally, partners did not always follow consistent tools, information, and standard practices, and the community struggled to leverage the food items that were being delivered, particularly those who had limited English proficiency.
As participants in the Morgan Stanley CodeToGive Hackathon, we were tasked with developing innovative solutions to these challenges.


Issues:

Atlanta Community Food Bank faced several challenges in ensuring frequent, consistent, and convenient delivery of equitable food to partners.

1. Partners had to order a minimum of 6,000 pounds of food for delivery, which sometimes led to missed deliveries or additional costs for renting vehicles.

2. Additionally, partners did not always follow consistent tools, information, and standard practices, and the community struggled to leverage the food items that were being delivered

3. Much of the community had limited English proficiency.


Solution:

1. Our team designed a clustering algorithm that creates clusters of partners near each other, whose orders sum up to more than 6,000 pounds. We also consider the delivery time window for each partner along with the ethnicities that each partner delivers food. This solution addresses the issue of convenient delivery of equitable food, even if the partner's order is less than 6,000 pounds. By doing so, we ensured that partners received the food they needed while optimizing the efficiency of delivery routes.

2. We also recognized the need for consistent tools, information, and standard practices among partners and the community. To address this issue, we developed a learning center that provides tutorials and details on different processes for partners. For the community, we created a learning center that covers nutritional values, recipes, and cooking tips to help people enjoy the food they receive. This approach will encourage the community to better understand the benefits of the food they receive, and how to incorporate it into their daily lives.

3. Finally, we implemented a multilanguage support feature that can be applied to the messaging architecture, inventory management, and other aspects of the system. This feature enables us to better serve people from different ethnicities, regions, and language backgrounds.



Our team designed a clustering algorithm that creates clusters of partners near each other, whose orders sum up to more than 6,000 pounds. We also consider the delivery time window for each partner along with the ethnicities that each partner delivers food. This solution addresses the issue of convenient delivery of equitable food, even if the partner's order is less than 6,000 pounds. By doing so, we ensured that partners received the food they needed while optimizing the efficiency of delivery routes.


Algorithm:

- Our algorithm uses KMeans from scikit-learn library for clustering geographic locations based on longitude, latitude, ethnicity, and delivery time window.
- It starts with 2 clusters and iterates up to a maximum of 8 clusters, trying to find the optimal number of clusters using silhouette score as the evaluation metric.
- It sets a minimum threshold for the number of orders per cluster(6000) and checks if any clusters have fewer orders than this threshold. If so, it increases the number of clusters to ensure that each cluster has at least minimum orders.
- The code keeps track of the best silhouette score and the corresponding number of clusters throughout the iterations and finally prints the optimal number of clusters with the highest silhouette score.
- Once the optimal number of clusters is found, it re-runs the KMeans algorithm with that number of clusters and assigns each location to a cluster. It then calculates the total number of orders per cluster and the centroids of each cluster.
- A visual representation of the cluster is shown using TSNE and Matplotlib

We also recognized the need for consistent tools, information, and standard practices among partners and the community. To address this issue, we developed a learning center that provides tutorials and details on different processes for partners. For the community, we created a learning center that covers nutritional values, recipes, and cooking tips to help people enjoy the food they receive. This approach will encourage the community to better understand the benefits of the food they receive, and how to incorporate it into their daily lives.
Finally, we implemented a multilanguage support feature that can be applied to the messaging architecture, inventory management, and other aspects of the system. This feature enables us to better serve people from different ethnicities, regions, and language backgrounds.


Algorithm:

- Our algorithm uses the googletrans library to translate text from one language to another.
- It defines a function that takes input text and target language as parameters and uses the Translator class to perform the translation.
- The translated text is displayed on the UI.


Future scope:

- Currently, there is no Maximum quantity mentioned for the orders, we can add that as a parameter based on the amount of food that can be delivered in one cluster and the size of the delivery trucks.
- Forecasting order quantity of the partners, using data analytics.
- Unattended delivery at the partner center using QR codes.


Overall, our Morgan Stanley CodeToGive Hackathon project provides an innovative solution to address the challenges faced by the Atlanta Community Food Bank. By leveraging technology and community outreach, we were able to create an effective and sustainable solution that improves the lives of those facing food insecurity. We believe that by working together and implementing innovative solutions, we can help create a more equitable and sustainable world.

In today’s digital age, social media platforms have become a common avenue for communication, but unfortunately, they have also become a breeding ground for hate speech and offensive language.

We have proposed an NLP-based approach using the Robustly Optimized BERT Approach (RoBERTa) that can detect not only explicit and direct forms of hate speech but also subtle and implicit forms. By taking into account the broader context in which the language is used, our model can better differentiate between language that is intended to be harmful and language that is not.

By analyzing online text, and data mining algorithms, we aim to identify patterns and features that differentiate between hate/offensive speech and non-hate speech to enable platforms to moderate content effectively and reduce the harm caused by hate speech also ensuring the safety and well-being of individuals using these platforms.


Data collection and aggregation:

We aggregated data from different data sources and social media sites.


Data Preprocessing & Exploratory Data Analysis:

- Handle missing values
- Data cleaning
- Handling contractions
- Data visualization using bar charts, word clouds, etc.


Algorithm and Methodology:

We tried different algorithms mentioned below:
1. LSTM
2. BERT
a. Distil-BERT
b. RoBERTa


Experiments and Results:

- LSTM has an accuracy of 86.36 % on the Validation set and 89.93 % on the testing set.

Featured - Machine Learning | CNN | Classification Object Detection

A classifier that decides the correct category of a given image



Object detection is a well-explored topic. In this project, the main task is to recognize the object in the images, i.e., to create a classifier that decides the correct category of a given image.


Dataset:

The training set contains 100 categories with labels from 0 to 99.
Each category has 5000 images.
Each image is formatted as 28 × 28 pixels.
There are 500K images in total.
The test set covers the same categories in the training set without any unseen categories. The test set has 100K images.


Data preprocessing:

Grayscale and Downsize
Each image has one object only.


Algorithm:

Many classification algorithms can be used, a few of them that I considered were:
• Logistic Regression
• Naiye Bayes
• K-Nearest Neighbors
• Decision Tree
• SVM
• CNN
Now from the above-mentioned algorithms, the best ones for Image classification are KNN and CNN
With KNN, the accuracy (44.48%) was not too good, so I decided to implement the project using CNN


Model design:

I implemented a CNN model to train the data
I used Keras API to implement it and stack the layers of my deep network
- Staring with a convolution layer, having 64 filters and each filter is 7 x 7
- The next layer is a max pool with a size of 2
- Then few more layers of convolution, adjusting the filters and their sizes to improve the accuracy
- This is followed by a flattened layer, which will take the output of the convolution and vectorize it, so it can pass through a multi-level perceptron or dense layers
- I have also used Dropout
- In the end, the output will have 100 units with activation as SoftMax


Results:

After training and fitting the model, I used 10 epochs
I got an accuracy of approximately 52%
To improve the accuracy, various regularization and optimization techniques can be used:
• Parameter sharing
• Weight decay
• Dropout
• Early stopping
• Change of learning rate during learning
• Adding momentum
• Dataset augmentation
• Activation function selection
